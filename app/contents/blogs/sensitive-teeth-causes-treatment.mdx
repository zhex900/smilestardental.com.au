Tooth sensitivity usually means dentin is exposed or nerves are irritated. The right fix depends on the cause—not every product at the pharmacy is equally useful.

## Frequent causes

- **Enamel wear** from acidic drinks, aggressive brushing, or grinding
- **Receding gums** exposing root surfaces
- **Decay or cracks** near the nerve
- **Recent whitening** or deep cleaning (often temporary)

## What your dentist may recommend

Desensitising toothpastes with potassium nitrate or stannous fluoride can help when enamel is thin. Fluoride varnish, bonding on exposed roots, or a night guard for grinding targets specific causes. If pain is sharp and lingering, rule out decay or nerve issues promptly.

## Habits that help

Use a soft brush, wait 30 minutes after acids before brushing, and keep regular checkups so small problems do not become painful ones.
